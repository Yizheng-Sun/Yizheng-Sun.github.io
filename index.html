Tristan Sun

Google ScholarEmail Tristan

Hi, I’m Tristan. I’m a PhD researcher in Computer Science at the University of Manchester, where I also earned my bachelor’s degree in Artificial Intelligence.

My PhD focuses on multimodal AI, particularly vision-language models. I study their computational efficiency, reliability, and robustness to better understand how they behave in practical settings.

My current research interests centre on AI agents and recursive self-improvement (RSI). I’m especially interested in how agent harnesses can learn from experience and improve their own workflows over time.

I use this space to share my thoughts, explore new ideas, and make sense of what I’m learning. Writing helps me work through complex concepts and explain them in a way that’s easy to follow. Alongside my blog posts, I keep personal learning notes on the concepts and experiments I’m exploring.

Paper ↗

Arg-LLaDA: Argument Summarization via Large Language Diffusion Models and Sufficiency-Aware Refinement

Uses a diffusion language model to revise debate summaries, checking for missing points, unsupported claims, and repetition to make them clearer and more faithful to the source.

EMNLP·2026

Paper ↗

Seeing What Is Actually There: PriVE-Bench and PriVE-Tools for Counterfactual Evaluation of Agentic Visual Evidence in VLMs

Tests whether vision-language models trust what an image shows over learned expectations, and finds that crops, zooms, and other visual tools help only in some settings.

AACL-IJCNLP·2026

Paper ↗

Are Reasoning Vision-Language Models Robust to Semantic Visual Distractions?

Introduces Distract-Bench and shows that irrelevant but plausible visual cues can derail a model’s reasoning, even when it can correctly perceive the evidence needed to answer.

Briefings in Bioinformatics·2026

Paper ↗

Foundation Models in Omics Research: A Comprehensive Survey

Reviews foundation models for molecular and cellular data, organizing approaches to biological sequences, cell states, and combined data sources alongside their applications and practical limitations.

Findings of NAACL·2025

Paper ↗

LVPruning: An Effective yet Simple Language-Guided Vision Token Pruning Approach for Multi-modal Large Language Models

Uses the text prompt to identify and remove less relevant image tokens, reducing computation while largely preserving performance and keeping the original model’s parameters unchanged.

NeurIPS·2025

Paper ↗

MIRA: Medical Time Series Foundation Model for Real-World Health Data

Builds a foundation model for forecasting medical measurements over time, handling irregular timestamps, different sampling rates, and missing values to transfer across diverse clinical datasets.

EMNLP·2025

Paper ↗

Does Acceleration Cause Hidden Instability in Vision Language Models? Uncovering Instance-Level Divergence Through a Large-Scale Empirical Study

Shows that speeding up vision-language models can change individual answers, including correct ones, even when overall accuracy barely changes—highlighting the need to check prediction stability.

Paper ↗

LanViKD: Cross-Modal Language-Vision Knowledge Distillation for Egocentric Action Recognition

Transfers a language model’s knowledge of relationships between actions into a video model, improving action recognition and generalization on first-person video benchmarks.
